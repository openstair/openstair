# OpenStair Blog Research Guidelines

## Purpose

Research exists to improve factual accuracy, technical depth, and reader value.

Research is not a mechanism for collecting text to rewrite.

---

# 1. Research when necessary

Research when claims are:

- current;
- version-dependent;
- technical and uncertain;
- security-related;
- standards-based;
- numerical;
- performance-related;
- historical;
- likely to have changed.

Do not research merely to make an article appear authoritative.

---

# 2. Source priority

Prefer:

## Tier 1 — Official primary sources

- official framework documentation;
- official language documentation;
- official API documentation;
- official specifications;
- official standards;
- official repositories;
- official release notes.

## Tier 2 — Primary technical sources

- original research papers;
- technical proposals;
- maintainer documentation;
- official engineering publications.

## Tier 3 — Reputable secondary sources

Use when primary sources do not sufficiently answer the question.

---

# 3. Research workflow

For each uncertain claim:

1. identify the exact claim;
2. determine whether verification is necessary;
3. locate the strongest appropriate source;
4. verify the claim;
5. understand its scope;
6. write the explanation independently;
7. add practical interpretation where useful.

Do not copy source structure.

---

# 4. Original synthesis

The article should transform research into useful understanding.

Useful synthesis can:

- explain why a documented behavior matters;
- compare alternatives;
- explain tradeoffs;
- connect multiple concepts;
- provide implementation context;
- explain practical consequences.

---

# 5. Citations

Never invent citations.

If the existing OpenStair blog system supports citations or references, follow the actual project convention.

If the project does not have a citation mechanism, do not invent one merely for appearance.

---

# 6. Numerical claims

Treat numerical claims as high risk.

Never invent:

- performance percentages;
- benchmarks;
- market statistics;
- adoption rates;
- costs;
- timings;
- survey results.

If a number is important:

- verify it;
- understand the measurement;
- preserve its scope;
- avoid universalizing context-specific results.

---

# 7. Software versions

For software-related claims:

- identify the relevant version where possible;
- consult current official documentation;
- distinguish historical from current behavior;
- avoid obsolete APIs;
- do not silently change an article's historical purpose.

---

# 8. First-hand experience

Never fabricate experience.

Do not write:

- "We tested this";
- "We measured this";
- "Our production system";
- "At OpenStair we found";

unless explicitly established by the repository or user.

Use appropriately general language instead.

---

# 9. Research limits

Stop research when:

- the factual uncertainty is resolved;
- authoritative evidence is sufficient;
- further research would not materially improve the article.

---

# 10. Anti-copying rule

Never:

1. select a competitor article;
2. copy its structure;
3. rewrite each paragraph;
4. change wording;
5. publish it as original.

Research should improve understanding, not provide a hidden article template.

---

# 11. Final research check

Before completion:

- Which important claims were verified?
- Did any unsupported fact enter the article?
- Did research improve the article?
- Is the resulting explanation independently written?
- Did the article add meaningful synthesis?
- Did any source-specific phrasing or structure accidentally survive?

If uncertain, revise.
